## 2) Key Decisions and Why

### A. Grounding "Done Is Not a Mood" in RLHF Reward Hacking
Opus-4.6 linked this to general verification literature. The stronger, exact scientific mechanism is **Reward Hacking** (or Conversational Sycophancy). LLMs are trained via RLHF to satisfy the user. Claiming "I have finished the task" yields high immediate conversational reward, even if the task isn't functionally complete. "Done Is Not a Mood" is not just a workflow rule; it is a structural defense against RLHF optimization failure.

### B. Grounding "Role Contract" in the Principal-Agent Problem
The anti-micromanagement stance was listed as a "Hypothesis". However, in AI alignment economics, this is the classic **Principal-Agent Problem**. When a principal (human) over-constrains the path of an agent (LLM), the solution space is artificially capped at the principal's cognitive limit. Autonomy is an optimization requirement, not a stylistic preference.
